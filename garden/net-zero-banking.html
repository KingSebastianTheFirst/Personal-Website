Net-Zero Banking

🌿 Growing

What it means when a bank commits to net-zero — and what the evidence says about whether commitments change behaviour.

📌 What is net-zero banking?

Net-zero banking refers to the commitment of banks to align their lending and investment portfolios with the goal of reaching net-zero greenhouse gas emissions — typically by 2050, in line with the Paris Agreement. The most prominent collective initiative is the UNEP FI Net-Zero Banking Alliance (NZBA), which brings together banks representing a substantial share of global banking assets.

The core claim of net-zero banking is that banks can contribute to global decarbonization not just through their own operational emissions (which are relatively small) but through the financed emissions embedded in their loan books and investment portfolios. A bank that finances coal plants or carbon-intensive industries at scale is effectively supporting continued emissions, regardless of how green its offices are.

💡 What does alignment mean in practice?

Net-zero portfolio alignment involves setting intermediate targets (typically for 2025 and 2030) for specific high-emitting sectors, measuring financed emissions, and steering the portfolio toward lower-emission counterparties. Banks can pursue alignment through:

  • Portfolio decarbonization: Reducing exposure to high-emission sectors and increasing financing of green activities.
  • Engagement: Working with existing clients to support their transition, rather than simply divesting.
  • Green product development: Offering sustainability-linked loans, green bonds, and transition finance products.
  • ESG integration in risk management: Incorporating climate risk into credit decisions and capital allocation.

🔬 In my research and advisory work

Before transitioning to full-time research, I spent several years at the UNEP Collaborating Centre supporting the development of net-zero banking frameworks and advising banks on climate risk integration. This practical experience directly shapes my research perspective.

My published policy report "Net Zero Banking in Practice: Climate Change in Banks' Core Business" (2024, Sustainable Finance Cluster) examines how German banks are integrating climate change into their core business — beyond sustainability departments and ESG products — and what distinguishes banks that are genuinely changing their lending practices from those still at the commitment stage.

The research on sustainable small business lending (published in Sustainable Futures, 2024) is directly connected: one of the biggest gaps in net-zero banking is the near-complete absence of SMEs from most banks' decarbonization plans, despite SMEs accounting for the majority of the real economy's emissions.

⚠️ Open questions

  • Do net-zero commitments actually change lending behaviour, or are they primarily a reputation management exercise?
  • How should banks handle high-emitting clients who are actively transitioning versus those who are not?
  • What is the right role for banks in supporting the transition of SMEs, where data and advisory capacity are both scarce?
  • How should financed emissions be measured and attributed — particularly for diversified bank portfolios with complex sector exposures?
