Sustainability Disclosure Design

🌱 Seedling

More data does not automatically mean better decisions. How information is presented shapes how it is understood.

🌱 Seedling note — This is an early-stage note connected to ongoing research. It will grow as the work develops.

📌 The problem

Sustainable finance regulation is increasingly focused on disclosure: requiring firms to report more, in more detail, on their sustainability performance. The implicit assumption is that better data leads to better decisions — by investors, lenders, regulators, and the public. But this assumption may be wrong, or at least incomplete.

Even if disclosure is complete and accurate, the information still needs to be processed and interpreted by human beings. And human beings have well-documented cognitive limitations — bounded attention, information overload, anchoring biases, and systematic errors in processing numerical data — that can lead to predictable mistakes even when the underlying data is good.

💡 What the research shows

In a large survey experiment (with Kornelia Fabisik, 2026), we find that individuals — including finance professionals and sustainability experts — systematically exhibit a central tendency bias when interpreting corporate carbon data. They tend to assume firms are roughly average in their emissions, leading them to overestimate the greenness of polluters and underestimate the greenness of clean firms. This bias does not disappear with expertise.

Importantly, however, we also find that some disclosure design features significantly improve differentiation ability. Specifically, presenting carbon data in relative terms — showing how a firm compares to sector peers or to a benchmark — helps participants distinguish green from brown firms more accurately.

This is a practically actionable finding for regulators designing mandatory disclosure templates: the format of disclosure matters, not just the content.

🌱 Open questions to develop

  • How do disclosure format effects interact with investor sophistication and domain expertise?
  • Does the finding extend to other sustainability metrics beyond carbon emissions?
  • How should regulators balance standardization (comparability) with design flexibility (interpretability)?
  • What is the role of financial intermediaries (asset managers, analysts) in reprocessing raw disclosure for retail investors?
