Responsible Investment & Decarbonization

🌿 Growing

Do ESG investors help decarbonize the real economy — or just their portfolios?

📌 What is responsible investment?

Responsible investment — often called ESG investing or sustainable investing — refers to the practice of incorporating environmental, social, and governance (ESG) considerations into investment decisions. The largest expression of this is the UN Principles for Responsible Investment (UN PRI), whose signatories now number over 5,000 and hold roughly one-third of all global equity.

Responsible investors operate under Article 2.1c of the Paris Agreement, which calls for making "financial flows consistent with a pathway toward low greenhouse gas emissions and climate-resilient development." Many have voluntarily committed to net-zero portfolio alignment through initiatives like GFANZ (Glasgow Financial Alliance for Net Zero) or Climate Action 100+.

💡 The key tension

There are two ways a responsible investor can try to contribute to decarbonization:

  • Capital reallocation: Tilt portfolios away from high-emission companies and toward green ones, raising the cost of capital for polluters.
  • Engagement: Use shareholder influence to push high-emission companies to change their business models and reduce actual emissions.

The critical question is: does either mechanism actually reduce emissions in the real economy, not just in portfolios? If investors simply reshuffle existing shares without inducing firms to change behaviour, the portfolio looks greener but the atmosphere is no better off.

🔬 In my research

In a working paper with Maurice Dumrose and Youri Matheis (2025), we use global institutional ownership data to examine whether UN PRI signatories contribute to real-economy decarbonization. The paper links responsible investor ownership to three outcomes at the firm level: capital allocation patterns, carbon reduction target commitments, and actual emission reductions.

Key findings:

  • Responsible investors allocate less capital to high-emission companies and more to already-green firms — consistent with a passive "consistency" interpretation of Paris alignment.
  • Higher responsible investor ownership is positively associated with a firm's likelihood of committing to carbon reduction targets.
  • However, responsible investor ownership does not predict actual emission reductions.
  • Instead, it is associated with improvements in ESG ratings — suggesting a focus on perceived sustainability over demonstrated decarbonization.

The paper's title — "Consistency or Transformation?" — reflects this tension between a passive, portfolio-level interpretation of Paris alignment and an active role in driving real change.
